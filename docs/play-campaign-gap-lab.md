# Campaign gap candidates

The original 21 snapshots remain unchanged. Thirteen primary candidates fill the missing slots, with fourteen alternatives available through a separate Candidate version selector. This preserves the 34-slot course outline. Next advances to the next primary slot, including after an alternative; alternatives have separate stable attempt identities, notes and replay snapshots.

The additional bank is generated by `scripts/build-campaign-gap-lab.ts` from the offline campaign-gap archive. It includes compact per-base comparison routes, shortest-found counts, support status, and the featured route. Each retained route is replayed against the unchanged live tolerance. Narrow shorter routes are retained alongside supported examples, not hidden by the support filter.

No existing par, pigment, mixing, control, camera or landing behavior changes. New holes receive provisional pars from the existing playerPar function and existing palette challenge profile (the existing 0.5 fallback for palettes without one); these are not a new par calibration. Source generation does not rerun the expensive palette search in the browser.

## Cautions

- Zorn cooling is relative cooling/muting with black, not a blue destination. The new finale has three-addition solutions from every base, but its approximately 1.09-tolerance two-addition near-misses fail the strict 1.10 safety margin. It is not labelled protected interior.
- CMY's primary has supported efficient ride examples from two of three paints. Magenta can avoid the ride. The greener alternative has more hue contrast but less ride coverage.
- Sienna's warm alternative is explicitly experimental: an efficient route includes a token-sized adjustment, so it fails that safeguard. It remains selectable for qualitative comparison.
- French Light's green and rose-gray candidates have supported solutions from every one of the eight starting paints after additional numerical three-dose exploration. Shortest counts differ by base. This measures route availability, not the cognitive challenge of selecting among eight paints.
- General audit success is not proof of shortest-route completeness, style invariance, enjoyment, or final course sequencing.

## French Light design hypothesis

Keep free starts for this lab. Its distinctive challenge may be selecting an economical paint subset from several plausible options. Useful future comparisons would measure per-base shot costs, distinct efficient ingredient subsets, substitutions that materially change the route, and the gap between a player's plausible initial choices and the best supported choices. More options can make planning harder while making physical reachability easier; these are separate effects.

Chained destinations would instead preserve the previous target's actual mixture and mass as the next state. That creates a different course-planning problem requiring whole-sequence testing: the same visible color can be reached with different pigment compositions and accumulated masses, changing subsequent options. Do not implement chaining or new rules as part of this publication.
