# Round 10 — inverse-planning portfolios

Thirty fixed holes across fifteen lab-only palettes, drawn from the 30 general-check survivors in the green and expanded portfolio archives. Failed candidates remain in research archives, not this playable collection. This is a review set, not a campaign sequence or claim that all holes are robust from all bases.

## Playing

In Hole Lab, select **New · 30 inverse-planning holes**. Choose any test palette and any hole. Start/replay a palette or use Replay hole. The campaign and earlier rounds remain selectable; existing attempts and review IDs are unchanged. New candidates append at palette index 31, leaving earlier indices intact.

Paints and intended experience are shown in the picker; full notes include per-base raw and timing-supported minima. Route review preserves the featured example, fewest-addition rival, closest-start example, and additional routes including raw witnesses. A raw rival can demand tighter timing than the featured route. All are sampled found routes, not proven optimal solutions.

## Selection

Every archived general-check survivor is included, even a single successful hole in a palette. No additional search, pigment substitutions, threshold adjustments, control changes or tolerance changes were made. Pars use the existing playerPar function with the found timing-supported minimum; the fewest-additions display retains the raw minimum.

Labels are exploratory intentions, not required routes. All-base two-addition candidates are called **Multi-start setup**, not the stricter three-addition interior class. Other labels select a retained ride, value-shift, balancing, glide or direct example. Availability and bypass counts are computed before reducing examples for display. The iteration varies style preference rather than claiming each palette follows an optimal fixed rhythm.

The Raw Umber quartet has three destinations but currently uses Umber principally as a starting paint, not an efficient added correction from other bases. Please note whether the palette actually gives Umber a worthwhile role. Hansa/Violet/Teal includes a two-addition found minimum from every base; this is a particularly useful free-start test.
